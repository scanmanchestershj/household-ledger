        How growth & dividend investing differ
        Growth stocks reinvest profits to expand the business rather than paying dividends — the return comes mainly from the share price rising over time. They tend to be more volatile and are usually judged on revenue/earnings growth rate, not current yield.

        Dividend stocks pay out a share of profit regularly, giving you cash income along the way — usually mature, stable companies. Look at dividend yield alongside the payout ratio (a very high payout ratio can be a warning sign it's not sustainable).
        What makes a stock "halal"
        Common Shariah screening (used by methodologies like AAOIFI, S&P Shariah, and MSCI Islamic) generally checks for:
        • Core business isn't in a prohibited sector — conventional banking/insurance, alcohol, gambling, pork, adult content, conventional weapons.
        • Interest-bearing debt stays under a set ratio of market cap (commonly ~30%).
        • Interest income and other impermissible income stay under a set ratio of total revenue (commonly ~5%), with that portion "purified" by donating it.
        Screening ratios vary by methodology and change as prices move, so a stock can drift in or out of compliance — dedicated screening tools (e.g. Zoya, Musaffa, Wahed's screens, or your broker's own Shariah filter) check this continuously; I can't verify current compliance for a specific stock here.
        This is general information, not financial or religious advice — for anything binding, a licensed financial advisor and a qualified scholar are the right calls.
